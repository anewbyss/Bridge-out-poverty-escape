Privacy Policy

Effective November 10, 2026 · Last updated November 10, 2026

Draft for review. Before launch, the organization’s legal name, address and contact person below must be filled in and this policy reviewed by a lawyer or legal aid clinic.

The short version

  • You don’t have to give your name. Every question is optional except agreeing to this policy.
  • We never sell or rent your information, and we don’t use it for advertising.
  • The “What do I qualify for?” screener saves nothing.
  • A private search keeps only your age, area, language and needs.
  • You can delete your record yourself at any time with your check-in code.
  • Staff are trained to keep what you share confidential. However, staff who work with young people must report child abuse under Oregon law (see “Limits to confidentiality” below).

Who we are

Bridge Out of Poverty (“we”, “us”) is a service-matching tool for young people ages 9–26 in the Portland, Oregon area. It is operated by [Organization legal name], [mailing address], Portland, Oregon. Privacy contact: [name / title], ripchoom@gmail.com.

What we collect

Search answers (optional)Age, ZIP code, county, language, and if you choose to share them: race/ethnicity, sex, gender identity, LGBTQIA2S+ status, family situation, household size, income range and sources, housing, school, work, safety and health situations, needs, preferences, and anything you write in notes.
Contact details (optional)A phone number or email, only if you check “it’s okay to contact me” or use Refer me, plus a first name or nickname if you give one.
Check-insWhat happened with each program, and what got in the way.
Screener answersNot saved. They are used once to show results, then discarded.
Technical dataYour IP address, kept in short-term memory to block attacks and abuse (for example, too many attempts). It is not linked to your answers. We don’t use advertising or analytics trackers, third-party cookies, or location tracking.
Staff accountsStaff name, work email, role, sign-in times and a log of actions taken in the staff tools.

Some of what you can share is sensitive data under Oregon and Washington law, such as health, sexual orientation, gender identity, immigration status, and being a victim of crime. We collect it only if you choose to share it, and only to find programs that fit you and to see which groups are being underserved.

How we use it

  • To match you with programs and show why each one fits.
  • To let you come back with your code, report how it went and refresh your matches.
  • To let staff follow up with you, but only if you asked them to.
  • To find gaps and barriers, such as needs with no program or groups that get help less often. Reports use totals only, and small counts are hidden when shared.
  • To keep the service safe: detecting and stopping misuse, attacks and fraud.

We don’t use your information to make automated decisions about you. Matches and screener results are suggestions only; programs make their own decisions.

Who can see it

  • Staff with a need to know. Each staff member has their own account. Case workers can see search records. Program staff (for example, a New Avenues for Youth program) see only Refer-me requests sent to their program, never your search answers. Every time staff open a record, it is logged.
  • The program you ask to be referred to. When you use Refer me, our staff contact you and, with your OK, help connect you to that program.
  • Email notifications. A Refer-me request sends a notice to our staff inbox (Google Gmail). By default that email does not include your name or contact details; staff sign in to see them.
  • Service providers that host the app or deliver email for us, under contracts that limit their use of the data.
  • When the law requires it: mandatory child-abuse reports, a valid court order or subpoena, or to prevent serious, imminent harm to someone. We will resist requests that aren’t legally valid. Where allowed, we tell you.

We don’t sell, rent or trade your information. We don’t share it for targeted advertising, and we don’t share it with immigration enforcement unless a valid court order compels it.

Limits to confidentiality

In Oregon, many people who work with young people are mandatory reporters (ORS 419B.005–419B.050). If you tell staff, directly or in your answers, that a child under 18 is being abused or neglected, staff may be required to report it to the Oregon Child Abuse Hotline (1-855-503-7233). Staff may also act if someone is in immediate danger. If you’re unsure, you can talk to a confidential crisis line first (988, Call to Safety 503-235-5333, or YouthLine).

Young people and parents

This service is made for young people 9–26. We don’t knowingly collect contact details from children under 13 unless a staff member or trusted adult is helping them. Under Oregon law, young people can get some services on their own (for example, mental health care at 14 and older, and reproductive health care at any age). We don’t share your answers with your parents or guardians unless the law requires it. A parent or guardian of a child under 13 may ask us to review or delete that child’s record.

How long we keep it

Search records and Refer-me requests are automatically deleted after [3 years] (staff can set a shorter period). Staff activity logs are kept for twice that period. You can delete your record sooner yourself.

How we protect it

  • Encrypted connections (HTTPS). Contact details and notes are also encrypted inside the database.
  • Personal staff accounts with strong passwords and two-step sign-in. Accounts lock after repeated failed sign-ins, and sessions end after 30 minutes of inactivity.
  • Least-privilege roles, audit logs, and the option to limit staff access to approved locations.
  • Protection against code guessing, cross-site attacks and automated spam.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your personal information, we will notify the people affected and regulators as required by Oregon law (ORS 646A.600–646A.628) and other applicable law. See our Security page.

Your rights and choices

Depending on where you live, laws such as the Oregon Consumer Privacy Act and Washington’s My Health My Data Act give you rights. We offer these rights to everyone:

  • See what we have about you, and get a copy.
  • Correct anything that’s wrong.
  • Delete it: on the Check in page, enter your code and choose “Delete my record”. You can also ask staff.
  • Withdraw consent to be contacted at any time.
  • Opt out of sale, targeted advertising and profiling. We don’t do any of these.
  • Appeal if we turn down a request. Write to the privacy contact above. If you disagree with the outcome, you can contact the Oregon Department of Justice (doj.state.or.us) or the Washington Attorney General.

To use a right, email ripchoom@gmail.com or ask any staff member. We answer within 45 days. Because we don’t collect names, we may ask for your check-in code to find and verify your record. We won’t treat you differently for using your rights.

Washington consumer health data

If you are in Washington (for example, Clark County), some answers such as health, mental health, substance use, pregnancy and gender-affirming care are “consumer health data” under the Washington My Health My Data Act. We collect it only with your consent, given by checking the agreement box. We use it only to match you with services, and share it only as described above. You can withdraw consent and delete it at any time. We do not sell consumer health data, and we do not set up geofences around health care providers.

Links to other programs

Program listings link to websites and phone numbers we don’t control. Those programs have their own privacy practices. Read them before you share information with them.

Changes

If we make important changes, we’ll update the date at the top and show a notice in the app. We won’t use information already collected in a materially different way without your consent.
